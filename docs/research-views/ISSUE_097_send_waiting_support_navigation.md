# SEND waiting, support and navigation: five-source research view

**Draft · LLM-assisted synthesis · needs_review · 5 November 2026.** For issue #97. This is a generated view of selected evidence, not a validated insight, authority ranking, prevalence estimate or linear journey. The interpretation and relationship judgements below require human review.

**Working picture:** formal-process timeliness, practical access to support and usable navigation are separate questions. They can fail together, but effective support and coordination can also coexist with waits. Staff confidence is a different outcome again. The table below makes those distinctions usable without treating all SEND difficulties as one navigation-burden object.

## A useful picture of the problem space

| Research question | Evidence-backed condition and variation | Analyst interpretation and practical research use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which wait is occurring? | Bexley: most new EHC plans on time but exceptions ([[EVID_006]], p. 2). Blackburn: therapy intervention waits up to 35 weeks ([[EVID_009]], p. 9). Essex: EHC needs-assessment timeliness appears in a wider dissatisfaction cluster ([[EVID_003_ofsted_essex_communication_support_available|EVID_003]], pp. 4–5). | **Keep the measures separate.** Issuing a plan, assessing needs and accessing therapy are different events; one generic waiting-time measure would obscure where support is blocked. Do not compare 20 weeks with 35 weeks as if they measured the same process. |
| Is practical help available while formal activity is incomplete or needs change? | Blackburn describes timely interim help after sudden changes ([[EVID_008]], p. 7) alongside therapy waits ([[EVID_009]]). Bexley describes coordination by professionals ([[EVID_007]], p. 5). | **A wait does not by itself establish absence of support.** Ask which support is available, for whom and at which trigger. The stronger claim that interim support mitigates the effects of therapy waits is untested because the report does not connect the groups. |
| Can people locate help and resolve questions? | Essex records support invisibility and unresolved questions/anxieties ([[EVID_002_ofsted_essex_support_not_visible|EVID_002]], p. 2; [[EVID_004_ofsted_essex_questions_anxieties_unresolved|EVID_004]], p. 5). Blackpool describes navigation difficulty and unsuitable information ([[EVID_010]], pp. 4–5). | A recurring **communication/orientation condition** is visible in these two local snapshots. It supports part of [[PP_002]], but not its whole burden, high severity, unpaid work or repeated-chasing formulation. Communication causing assessment delay is not established. |
| What happens during waiting? | Blackpool reports family accounts of escalating needs and difficulty coping in the delay paragraph ([[EVID_011]], p. 3); Essex connects dissatisfaction with a cluster of waits and communication ([[EVID_003_ofsted_essex_communication_support_available|EVID_003]]). | Preserve **reported/source-attributed consequence**, rather than making it an independently demonstrated causal chain. The hypothesis that unreliable orientation compounds waiting-related harm is useful for follow-up, but its magnitude, sequence and mechanism remain untested. |
| Who performs coordination? | Bexley professionals coordinate appointments ([[EVID_007]]); Blackburn professionals assemble interim support ([[EVID_008]]). Blackpool families report navigation difficulty ([[EVID_010]]). | Coordination can be carried by professionals or become difficult for families. These are **different configurations**, not proof that all families always carry the system. This comparison qualifies a universal burden story; it does not invalidate the conditional pain point. |
| Do professional improvements translate into timely help? | RR1589 reports 75% agreement about identification confidence, question base n=990 ([[EVID_012]], pp. 62–63). The evaluator did not measure changes in pupil outcomes and had no suitable causal comparator ([[EVID_013]], pp. 14, 21–25). | Staff confidence is a positive signal with a different unit of analysis. It does not show faster identification, fewer EHC delays or better child outcomes. Avoid presenting training as an established solution to the local waiting problems. |

These questions can coexist in one family’s situation and recur across education, health and care. This view makes no claim about a common sequence, a uniform pathway or a fixed set of stages. A family may receive useful help while another service remains inaccessible.

## What can be used now, and what remains missing

This view is useful for research planning: separate process completion, access to help, information/contact continuity, actor work and experienced impact when comparing a case or designing the next interview. It does not prioritise a product or prescribe a dashboard, named worker or training intervention.

The immediate follow-up questions are grounded in the gaps above:

- For families who are waiting, what practical action are they trying to take, what help do they already receive, and what remains blocked? This would test the action/context of UN_015 rather than treating delay as a need.
- Which contacts, attempts, hand-offs and outcomes actually occur? Capture first-person examples to distinguish routine follow-up, repeated chasing and formal challenge before linking BEH_009.
- For services with interim arrangements, who is reached and who is not? Neither positive inspection example provides comparative coverage or a measured effect on harms from delay.
- Do reported staff capability gains correspond to independently observed earlier identification or support? RR1589 cannot answer that causal question.

The apparent evidence gaps are therefore **direct family/young-person action and capacity, distribution of access, comparative outcomes and tested causal mechanisms**. They are not proof that those phenomena or supporting research do not exist in the remaining corpus.

## Did CDI work in this test?

- **Meaning and provenance:** yes, at a reviewable level. Eight bounded records retain report version, source voice, pages, short wording, qualifiers and SHA-256 links. No LLM text is represented as participant evidence.
- **Reconciliation:** yes. Five Essex records were reused, including the EHCNA clause already in EVID_003. PP_002 received partial/limiting relations from the new evidence without changing its meaning or maturity; mismatched need/behaviour links were deferred.
- **Variation and uncertainty:** yes, in prose and tables. Positive professional coordination is visible alongside service waits, and staff confidence is separated from child outcomes. These are qualifications, not experimentally demonstrated contradictions.
- **Useful view:** yes for inspecting the problem space and choosing research questions; insufficient for intervention effectiveness, prevalence or authority benchmarking.
- **Friction exposed:** the generic `related_*` fields do not distinguish support from qualification without reading prose; source and evidence-map navigation across repositories is manual; existing EVID_003 has a page-span/title mismatch; the validator cannot verify quote fidelity, causal boundaries, external links or question denominators. No architecture change was needed. Human judgement remains the main work.
